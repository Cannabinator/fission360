# Closing Fission360's gap to Fusion 360

Fission360 already has the core of a parametric solid modeller, so its path to credibility is a short list of high-leverage additions plus a trust-and-openness positioning that Autodesk cannot copy. The cheapest wins are modelling completeness: sketch text, threads, sketch offset/mirror/pattern tools, variable-radius and full-round fillets, DXF/SVG/image import, and 3MF export. The next tier is the one that separates a toy from a tool: components with joints and assembly-preserving STEP, 2D drawings with DXF output, a Python scripting API that shares the GUI's document model, and a robustness layer around OpenCASCADE fillets, shells and booleans. Sheet metal, 2.5D CAM and linear FEA are real differentiators for shops but are large builds that should follow, and T-splines, generative design, electronics and cloud PDM should be deferred or skipped. The market opening is real but documented only thinly: Fusion's free tier has been cut repeatedly, commercial use costs about $680/yr, there is no Linux client, and cloud dependence is a recurring complaint. Competitors show where the bar sits. Onshape wins on versioning and FeatureScript, SolidWorks on drawings and large assemblies, Plasticity and Shapr3D on direct-edit UX, and FreeCAD on being free but fragmented. Most evidence behind this report comes from search-result snippets because every page fetch failed, so the ranked gap list is analyst judgement rather than measured demand; the final section lists what to verify first.

## Fusion's footprint: what is core, what is paid, and where Fission360 stands

Fusion's modelling surface is wide, and Autodesk is still extending it. The Create menu lists **Rib, Web, Emboss, Hole, Thread and Boundary Fill** among its solid tools ([Autodesk help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Model/files/SLD-CREATE-SOLID-FROM-SKETCH.htm)). Rib and Web build thin features from an open profile extruded to the nearest solid faces ([Autodesk blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/fusion-360-in-depth-web-command/)), Emboss raises or recesses a profile on a face ([Autodesk help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Model/files/SLD-CREATE-SOLID-FROM-SKETCH.htm)), and threads default to cosmetic with a modeled option in document settings ([Autodesk tech tip](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/tech-tip-threading-parts-in-fusion-360/)). The 2026 cadence shows where Autodesk is heading: a hub-based Threads Library and construction-geometry upgrades in July ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/july-2026-major-product-update-whats-new/)), a natural-language Autodesk Assistant in April ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/april-2026-product-update-whats-new/)), and a September release (v2705.1.11) promising speed, more predictable lofts, expanded sheet metal and PMI, and scene lights ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/september-2026-major-product-update-whats-new/)). Reviewers praise the integrated CAD, CAM, simulation and cloud version control, and criticise slowness and weak organic modelling ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/178416/Fusion-360/reviews/), [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/fusion-360)).

The paid structure matters for deciding what to copy. Extensions gate the heavy end: the Manufacturing Extension adds 4/5-axis machining, nesting, metal additive and advanced probing ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/manufacturing-extension.html)); the Simulation Extension adds nonlinear, buckling, modal, thermal and event studies with unlimited cloud solving ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/in/products/fusion-360/simulation-extension)); the Design Extension adds material-driven design rules, plastic features and lattices ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/design-extension.html)). By inference, the core subscription includes parametric design, surface, mesh, drawing, sheet metal, basic CAM and basic linear simulation, so a competitor must at least cover the core tier; the notes did not confirm the exact core-versus-extension boundary from Autodesk's pages.

## What users actually complain about, and what they say they want

The documented pain points cluster around trust rather than missing commands. Cloud dependence comes up repeatedly: a user explained that Fusion's cloud-only design blocks them from offline commercial work, and Autodesk's answer was that it is cloud-based by design ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/eagle-forum/eagle-dying-on-june-7-2026/m-p/12952477/highlight/true)). A 3 August 2023 database failure forced Fusion into offline mode until midday Pacific ([Autodesk health](https://health.autodesk.com/incidents/zf4656lzpl6v)), and earlier outages blocked open, save and export ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-design-validate-document/resolved-fusion-360-outage/m-p/6910941)). Hobbyists objected to the 10-editable-document cap, which one said discourages splitting components into separate files ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-support-forum/please-autodesk-don-t-limit-the-personal-licenses/td-p/9750422)). Users also reported fragile references: in a roughly 1,000-operation design, references far back in the timeline broke without edits, and repairing warnings cascaded into a day of work ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-support-forum/references-repeatedly-lost/m-p/13141717/highlight/true)). Another user said sketches "break in very strange ways," with tangents flipping and projections lost, and cited Onshape's sketches as more robust ([V1 Engineering forum](https://forum.v1engineering.com/t/time-for-a-change-of-cad/31203)). This matters for Fission360 because topological naming is a differentiator even against Fusion, not just against FreeCAD, and Fission360's geometric-signature references are the right instinct if they come with a visible re-link and repair UI. Fusion's offer to re-link projected geometry when upstream changes is something users specifically value over Onshape's manual re-link ([V1 Engineering forum](https://forum.v1engineering.com/t/time-for-a-change-of-cad/31203)).

What users love sets the minimum bar: the components-sketches-bodies-timeline model that one maker called the key to using Fusion for both CNC and 3D printing ([Sienci forum](https://forum.sienci.com/t/someone-school-me-on-fusion-vs-onshape/8214)), fast constraint UX (a FreeCAD switcher found FreeCAD's tools clunky and much slower), and Onshape's timeline preview when editing earlier features ([V1 Engineering forum](https://forum.v1engineering.com/t/time-for-a-change-of-cad/31203)). On the request side, the best sourced data point is historical: sheet metal was the top-voted IdeaStation idea in 2016 and later shipped ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/fusion-forecast-sheetmetal/)). A Linux port is a long-standing request with 253 comments and "Future Consideration" status ([Autodesk IdeaStation](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/ideastation-request-a-feature-or/linux/idi-p/4530727)). Users also asked for mesh sculpting inside Fusion, which Autodesk's product manager called useful but not an active focus ([Autodesk Feedback Hub](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-360-feedback-hub/open-looking-for-feedback-on-mesh-based-workflows/m-p/11271796)). The legacy IdeaStation is archived, so no current ranked request list exists in the notes. Maker-specific asks such as threads, text, tolerances and 3MF quality, plus performance and crash sentiment, were not sourced; the ranking of those features here is inference.

## Pricing and licensing: a real opening, with shaky numbers

Autodesk's commercial plan rose to **$85/month, $680/year and $2,040 for three years on 30 January 2024**, when "Fusion 360" was renamed "Autodesk Fusion"; existing annual subscribers were price-locked at $490 until a 2027 renewal ([Autodesk blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/?p=63902), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodesk_Fusion), [Symetri](https://www.symetri.co.uk/news-events/news/upcoming-changes-to-fusion-360/)). Earlier increases are documented (a 3-year plan from US$1,410 to US$1,555 ([Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/news/autodesk-to-raise-fusion-360-pricing))), and 2026 bundle prices of $2,040/yr for Fusion for Manufacturing and $2,190/yr for Fusion for Design come only from third-party guides ([mfgrobots](https://www.mfgrobots.com/Article/mfg/it/53531.html), [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/autodesk-fusion/pricing)). No Autodesk-hosted 2026 price page was retrieved, so treat these as unverified.

The free personal tier has been narrowed in steps. Autodesk's own pages restrict it to individuals earning **under $1,000/year**, not for primary employment or company use ([Autodesk](https://autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/personal)), with a three-year term that must be reapplied for ([Autodesk blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/?p=18687)). The 10-editable-document cap took effect 19 January 2021 and Autodesk says STEP export remains ([Autodesk support](https://autodesk.com/support/technical/article/Fusion-360-Free-License-Changes)). The 2020 cuts, per customer emails quoted on forums, removed probing, multi-axis milling, simulation and generative design ([Snapmaker forum](https://forum.snapmaker.com/t/fusion-360-features-removed/9392), [OneFinity forum](https://forum.onefinitycnc.com/t/major-changes-to-fusion-360-for-hobby-use/874)). Conflicting claims exist: the same forum quotes list STEP export as removed while Autodesk's support page says it stays, and a third-party site cites a lower income cap and other terms that Autodesk's pages do not repeat ([Stacksheriff](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/fusion-360-free-personal-license/)). The pragmatic reading: **the gap between hobbyist ($0) and commercial ($680/yr) is the pressure point**, and a seller of printed parts crossing $1,000/yr hits it (inference).

Alternatives set the price anchors. SolidWorks for Makers costs about **$48/yr** for hobbyists under $2,000 revenue but requires Windows ([SolidWorks](https://www.solidworks.com/solution/solidworks-makers/thingiverse.md), [All3DP](https://all3dp.com/1/solidworks-free-download/)); a $24/yr promotion was reported by a forum user ([Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/solidworks-for-makers-is-now-only-24-year/115575)). Onshape's free plan allows unlimited public documents but no private ones and no commercial use ([Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/it/blog/understand-your-cad-plan)); paid Onshape is reported at roughly $1,500 to $2,500/yr, with sources disagreeing ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/onshape), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com.sg/software/164681/onshape)). Plasticity sells perpetual licences with 12 months of updates, commercial use allowed ([Plasticity docs](https://doc.plasticity.xyz/getting-started/license-types-and-features)); one source gives the Indie tier as $149 and another as $175, with Studio at $299 in both ([Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/?p=222842), [Plasticity](https://www.plasticity.xyz/buy)). Shapr3D prices disagree across five sources, from about $21/month to $37.99/month ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/shapr3d), [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/shapr3d/pricing)). Inventor sits near $2,500/yr with no personal tier ([ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-autodesk-inventor-cost/)).

Linux is the cleanest unserved niche. Autodesk's community manager has stated Linux is unsupported ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/design-validate-document/does-autodesk-fusion-360-support-linux-os/td-p/6565836)), and users rely on fragile Wine/Proton installers ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/fusion-360-for-linux-how-to-install-it/)), Onshape in a browser, or FreeCAD ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/fusion-360/)). No source documents AI-training terms, login mandates or data-ownership fears as migration triggers, so those should not appear in marketing claims without further research. On sustainability, FreeCAD took in only **EUR 129,981 in donations in 2025 against EUR 127,094 spent** ([FreeCAD Project Association](https://fpa.freecad.org/annualreports/2026.html)), which shows donations alone will not fund a full team; a hybrid of sponsorship and optional paid services is the realistic path (inference supported by Blender's patron model ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/tag/blender-development-fund/))).

## Competitors: what each does well, what it costs, and what to copy

FreeCAD is the open-source incumbent and its recent history is the best guide to what to avoid. Version 1.0 (18 November 2024) shipped topological-naming mitigation and an integrated Assembly workbench; maintainers said the naming fix is "not completely solved" and applies only to Sketcher and PartDesign ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/freecad-1-0-new-features-and-the-larger-picture/46690)). Version 1.1 (24 March 2026; one article says 25 March) added datums usable in any workbench, transparent previews of additive and subtractive features, on-canvas draggers, and a Clarify Selection tool ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/3/freecad-1-1-adds-three-point-lighting-part-design-previews-and-major-fem-upgrades), [OSArch](https://osarch.org/2026/03/25/freecad-1-1-released/)). Its Assembly workbench was written by the Ondsel team with its own solver ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/blog/page/780/?cat=LDR)). The 2025 annual report acknowledges community turmoil including developer departures ([FreeCAD blog](https://blog.freecad.org/2026/04/24/2025-annual-report-and-plans-for-2026/)). The inference is that FreeCAD pays down UX debt slowly and a focused product can out-iterate it, but complaints cited are dated anecdotes (2023 to 2024) and low weight ([Lemmy mirror](https://feddit.it/post/3978460/4353488)). One G2 comparison rates Fusion higher overall and FreeCAD better on drawings and large models ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/autodesk-fusion-vs-freecad)).

The small open projects show what a lean team can do. Dune3D combines SolveSpace's solver workflow with OpenCASCADE; its author left FreeCAD over toponaming and a modal 2D-only sketcher with no 3D constraints ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/tag/dune3d/)). Version 1.3 added bitmap import, pipe and mirror groups, curvature combs and constraint preview; 1.4 added DXF export and a navigation cube ([Flathub](https://flathub.org/az/apps/org.dune3d.dune3d), [relayed announcement](https://hackers.pub/@karotte@chaos.social/019c0705-34a1-763a-8db3-6968b8ed6ae3/shares)). SolveSpace itself is GPL-3.0 with its last stable release (3.1) dated 1 June 2022 ([SolveSpace](https://solvespace.com/download.pl)). Its libslvs library is reused by Dune3D and Blender's CAD Sketcher, but a GPL-3 solver constrains any non-GPL Fission360. CadQuery and build123d prove a Python layer on OCP is cheap leverage: build123d is described as CadQuery's evolution with explicit algebra instead of fluent chaining ([build123d docs](https://build123d.readthedocs.io/_/downloads/en/latest/epub/)), and users found CadQuery hard to reason about because of state tracking ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/threads?id=wiw2)). Their gap is the absence of a GUI timeline, which is exactly what a Fission360 API can supply. One aggregator lists CadQuery as GPL-3.0-or-later while background knowledge says Apache-2.0; that is unresolved and should be checked in the repository.

Commercial tools define the quality ceiling. Onshape runs Parasolid and D-Cubed on servers and sends triangles to the browser ([Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/how-does-onshape-really-work)). Its branch/merge works per tab (target, source or combined), cannot cherry-pick within a tab, and is blocked across different FeatureScript versions ([Onshape help](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Document/merging.htm)); an older forum thread criticises the lack of conflict resolution ([Onshape forum](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/1353/versions-and-branching)). FeatureScript's strength is that built-ins such as Extrude and Fillet are written in the same language users get ([FeatureScript guide](https://cad.onshape.com/FsDoc/intro.html)). SolidWorks is praised for complex assemblies, simulation and detailed drawings, though the sources are partly reseller-biased ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/fusion-360-vs-solidworks-cad-software-compared-side-by-side/?critical=1), [GoEngineer](https://www.goengineer.com/en-us/onshape-vs-solidworks-jotform)). Shapr3D on Parasolid is fast to learn, but reviewers cite parametric-history friction, slowness with large multi-body models and lighter drawings and assemblies ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/Shapr3D/reviews), [Develop3D](https://develop3d.com/reviews/review-shapr3d-ipad-parasoild-modelling/)). Plasticity draws praise for direct-edit UX, but fillet reliability reports are mixed even on Parasolid ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/robust-fillets-interacting-and-stable-in-plasticity/159922)). The inference worth acting on is that Shapr3D's retrofit of parametrics proved painful, so Fission360 should expose direct edits as history operations from the start rather than as a separate mode.

## Kernel and performance: stay on OpenCASCADE, build the robustness layer

OpenCASCADE is now at 8.0.x, with 7.9.x maintained alongside. Version 8.0.0 brought 500+ changes, a redesigned geometry-evaluation API, a C++17 baseline, std threading and Gordon surfaces ([OCCT 8.0.0](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/open-cascade-technology-800-release), [RC5](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/open-cascade-technology-800-release-candidate-5)); 8.0.1 notes focus on modelling reliability, shape healing, meshing, STEP export and concurrent fillet construction ([release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/release/V8.0.1)). Whether 8.0 improves fillet robustness is unverified. A product on the OCP Python bindings lags: the current cadquery-ocp is 7.9.3.x, built on OCCT 7.9.2 libraries ([piwheels](https://www.piwheels.org/project/cadquery-ocp), [Debian](https://packages.debian.org/sid/ppc64/python3-ocp)), with a no-VTK variant extending Python support to 3.14 ([ocp-build-system](https://github.com/CadQuery/ocp-build-system)). Fission360, with its own renderer, should prefer the no-VTK wheel (inference).

No published head-to-head fillet benchmark exists in the notes. The evidence is anecdotal: practitioners rank Parasolid and Autodesk's kernel as having few fillet failures ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/best-software-to-make-fillets-exporting-from-rhino/47429)), and a 2024 paper found a new Boolean algorithm faster than OCCT and roughly on par with Parasolid and ACIS ([JCAD 2024](https://www.jcad.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2024-00367.pdf)). The analyst conclusion is that kernel robustness is Fission360's main commercial risk and that **failure mitigation (tolerance retries, ShapeFix, clear diagnostics, live preview of fillet feasibility)** is a feature in its own right. Kernel alternatives do not rescue this: Parasolid is licensed to 130+ ISVs with no public price ([engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/parasolid-d-cubed-and-siemens-the-heart-of-your-cad-software-belongs-to-another/)), Fornjot was archived on 19 June 2026 with its goals unmet ([GitHub](https://github.com/hannobraun/fornjot)), and truck's robustness is unknown ([GitHub](https://github.com/ricosjp/truck)). A kernel-abstraction layer with Fission360's own shape and face IDs keeps a later swap possible (inference).

Performance guidance is largely inferred. Sourced facts are that OCCT mesh generation can be parallel via TBB ([OCCT forum](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/multithreaded-brepmeshmesh)), with a report of up to 10x speedup in one 93,000-box test ([refactoring BRepMesh](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/refactoring-brepmesh)), but older reports of thread-safety crashes exist and current status is unverified ([OCCT forum](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/occt-run-separate-threads-multithreading-too-slow)). A tracker report also shows a meshing regression between 7.6.1 and 7.8.0 for a conical surface ([Mantis 33681](https://tracker.dev.opencascade.org/view.php?id=33681)). Because Python's GIL limits threading, running kernel work in worker processes and caching tessellation per shape hash is a reasonable design (inference, not benchmarked).

Licensing is mostly manageable. OCCT 6.7.0 and later is LGPL-2.1 with an exception allowing object-code distribution under your own terms ([SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/OCCT-exception-1.0.html)); PySide6 is LGPLv3/GPL/commercial and QtCharts is GPL-only ([Python GUIs](https://www.pythonguis.com/faq/licensing-differences-between-pyqt6-and-pyside6/)); OCP is Apache-2.0 but its wheels bundle native libraries whose licences travel with them ([conda-forge](https://anaconda.org/conda-forge/ocp)). GPL components such as Gmsh are best run as separate processes if the product is not GPL ([Gmsh](https://www.gmsh.info)); OpenCAMLib moved to LGPL in 2018 ([Debian](https://metadata.ftp-master.debian.org/changelogs/main/o/opencamlib/stable_copyright)). A licence decision for Fission360 itself comes first because libslvs (GPL-3) and Gmsh depend on it. This is not legal advice.

On topological naming, the research found no source describing how Onshape or Parasolid persist IDs, and FreeCAD's fix remains partial. The design that fits Fission360's existing architecture, per the notes' inference, is to record per-feature maps from input sub-shapes to outputs using OCCT's Modified/Generated/IsDeleted history, store references as feature ID plus generation role plus index, and keep the geometric signature (surface type, area, centroid, normal) as a fallback with a repair dialog for ambiguous cases. That extends what Fission360 has rather than replacing it.

## Prioritized roadmap tied to Fission360's current feature set

The sequencing rule is: finish the modelling vocabulary that every user hits first, then add the structures that make multi-part products possible, then the shop-grade outputs, and only then the large vertical builds. Effort is in rough person-months for one experienced developer, an inference from the notes' 1 to 5 difficulty scale rather than a sourced estimate; impact reflects the stated importance for hobbyist, startup and shop users.

**Phase 0, foundation (do alongside Phase 1).** Choose a licence (see above) and keep dynamic linking for OCCT and PySide6. Harden the document model for what comes next: stable feature IDs and a human-readable, diffable project format so Git and later branch/merge work, building on the declarative feature-spec architecture. Add a failure-mitigation layer around fillet, shell, offset and boolean (ShapeFix, tolerance retry, plain-language errors, visible re-link UI for lost references). Effort about 2 months; impact is high because every later feature inherits it.

**Phase 1, close the vocabulary gap (about 3 to 4 months, very high impact).** Sketch text with extrude, cut and emboss (OCCT provides a text builder), then sketch offset, mirror, rectangular and circular pattern, ellipse, extend, break, stretch and sketch-on-any-face. Add variable-radius and full-round fillets, cosmetic threads linked to Hole with ISO/UNC tables, canvas image, SVG and DXF import for sketches, 3MF export, and DXF sketch export. Add Rib/Web, thicken and stitch as thin layers on existing extrude, shell and combine. These are the items the notes rank highest on importance per unit difficulty and they map directly onto Fission360's current sketch, hole, fillet and pattern features. Also include UX features other tools show are cheap and loved: live feature preview, on-canvas draggers, constraint preview with remaining degrees of freedom, a navigation cube, Clarify Selection and unified datums ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/3/freecad-1-1-adds-three-point-lighting-part-design-previews-and-major-fem-upgrades), [Flathub](https://flathub.org/az/apps/org.dune3d.dune3d)).

**Phase 2, enable products and teams (about 6 to 8 months, high impact).** Components with per-component location and a tree, assembly-preserving STEP import and export via OCCT's XCAF, four to five joint types (rigid, revolute, slider, cylindrical, ball) with limits, ground, as-built joint and drag testing. The joint solver is not in OCCT and must be written (inference). Add a Python scripting API that exposes the same document model so scripted features appear in the timeline, built-ins use it, and a headless mode exists for CI; follow build123d's explicit style. Add the direct-edit set (offset, move, delete, replace and split face) recorded as history operations, and a first drawing module using OCCT hidden-line removal for orthographic and section views, dimensions, BOM and DXF/PDF output (difficulty 3 to 4). Add file-based versioning: autosaved operation log, named snapshots, and later a feature-tree-level three-way merge, which would improve on Onshape's whole-tab merging (inference). Impact: high, since these gate multi-part and professional use.

**Phase 3, shop and showcase features (about 8 to 12 months, medium-high impact).** Sheet metal with flanges, bends, unfold and flat-pattern DXF using a K-factor model (no OCCT support, difficulty 4); this was the top-voted Fusion request in 2016 ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/fusion-forecast-sheetmetal/)) and matters most to shops. Rendering through an existing path tracer rather than a custom one; configurations (table-driven variants); exploded views and BOM; curvature, draft and zebra analysis; modeled threads with fallback to cosmetic; surfacing tools (patch, trim, extend, offset curve). Mesh repair and reduction via existing libraries, with mesh-to-BRep deferred.

**Phase 4, vertical builds (12+ months, conditional on traction).** 2.5D CAM (adaptive, pocket, contour, drill) using OpenCAMLib (LGPL) and Clipper-class libraries, with tool libraries and a small set of post-processors; this is large, but integrated CAM is the reason makers stay on Fusion, and Fusion fences multi-axis and advanced probing behind a paid extension ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/manufacturing-extension.html)). Linear static FEA via a Gmsh and CalculiX process pipeline. Defer or skip T-spline sculpting (patented, difficulty 5), generative design, native electronics (Fusion is deepening Altium import, so interchange via STEP or IDF is the realistic answer) and cloud PDM; the notes recommend git-like file versioning as the lightweight alternative.

Strongest evidence: Autodesk's own pages and blogs for the existence of features, extension contents, the $1,000 personal-use revenue limit, the 10-document cap and the 30 January 2024 price change; OCCT and FreeCAD release notes and annual reports; the FeatureScript and Onshape merge documentation. Weakest evidence: every ranking of command importance, every difficulty score, every effort estimate, any claim about Fusion's core-versus-extension boundary beyond the extension pages, and the performance architecture advice. All of these are inference.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that Fission360's biggest competitive risks are not feature counts but two things the notes keep returning to: reference and kernel robustness, and trust. Fusion users themselves report lost references and broken sketches, FreeCAD's fix covers only part of its codebase, and even Parasolid-based Plasticity gets mixed fillet reviews, so a visible repair UI and fillet diagnostics can be a headline differentiator rather than plumbing. On trust, the combination no competitor offers in the sourced evidence is native Linux, local files, an unrestricted commercial licence and a Fusion-like timeline. That is a positioning claim, not a measured market, and the evidence for it is a handful of forum threads and one 253-comment Linux request.

The implication for sequencing is to ship Phase 1 fast to remove "missing basics" objections, then spend the scarce engineering effort on the three multipliers that no competitor in the open space has combined: a script-able document model shared with the GUI, a diffable file format that makes Git a credible alternative to cloud versioning, and an assembly and drawing core. CAM and sheet metal are the features that would retain shop users, but they should be committed to only after the first multi-part, scripted, drawing-capable release shows traction.
